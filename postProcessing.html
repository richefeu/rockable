Post-processing (postpro)

Where the data extractors measure things during a computation, postpro works afterwards, by re-reading the conf-files that were saved. This is the right tool for quantities that are too expensive to evaluate at every step, or that were not anticipated when the run was launched.

postpro commands.txt

Note

postpro is built when the CMake option ROCKABLE_COMPILE_POSTPRO is on, which is the default.

The command file

The command file selects a range of conf-files and one post-processor:

commands.txt
firstConf 0
lastConf 100
stepConf 5

PostProcessor ParticleStress
Volume 1.0

Keyword

Description

firstConf <int>

Number of the first conf-file to process.

lastConf <int>

Number of the last conf-file to process (inclusive).

stepConf <int>

Increment between two processed conf-files.

PostProcessor <name>

Selects the post-processor. The keywords that follow, until the end of the file, are its own parameters.

The conf-files are loaded one after the other, from ./conf<first> to ./conf<last>, and the post-processor is executed on each of them.

Warning

postpro loads the configurations in interactive mode. The data extractors declared in dataExtractors.txt are therefore not run again, and nothing is written back into the conf-files.

Important

Only one post-processor can be used per command file. Run postpro twice, with two command files, to obtain two different analyses.

ParticleStress

Computes, for each processed configuration, the tensorial moment of each particle,

\[\mathbf{M}_i = \sum_c \underline{F}_c \otimes \left( \underline{x}_i - \underline{x}_c \right)\]

where the sum runs over the interactions of the particle, \(\underline{F}_c\) is the force it receives at the contact point \(\underline{x}_c\), and \(\underline{x}_i\) is its centre (the nearest image with a periodic cell). It is positive in compression. The driven bodies are skipped. The sum of the \(\mathbf{M}_i\) divided by the volume is the stress tensor of the sample, the same as the one of SampleState.

Note

Up to October 2026, the absolute position \(\underline{x}_c\) was used instead of \(\underline{x}_i - \underline{x}_c\). The result was the same for a particle in equilibrium only, and depended on the origin otherwise: on a sample compressed by a servo-control, the stress of the whole sample could be wrong by a factor of two.

The results are written into ParticleStress-conf<iconf>.txt, one line per particle: the 9 components of \(\mathbf{M}_i\) (they are not divided by the volume of the particle), then four values that separate an axial part, along the axis \(z\) of the body (\(\underline{u}\)), from a radial part: \(a = \underline{u} \cdot \mathbf{M}_i \underline{u}\), \(r = \sqrt{r_1^2 + r_2^2}\) with \(r_k = \underline{t}_k \cdot \mathbf{M}_i \underline{t}_k\) for two unit vectors \(\underline{t}_1, \underline{t}_2\) perpendicular to \(\underline{u}\), then the trace of the axial part and the trace of the radial part. The stress of the whole sample, \(\sum_i \mathbf{M}_i / V\), is printed on the terminal.

Keyword

Description

Volume <double>

Total volume used to normalise the stress of the sample. This is the default value, used for every configuration that is not listed below.

ConfVolumes <nb>

Followed by nb lines <iconf> <volume>, giving the volume of specific configurations.

The volume has to be provided because Rockable has no general way of knowing the volume actually occupied by a sample: it depends on the boundaries, which may be walls, a periodic cell, or nothing at all. When the sample is compacted during the run, its volume changes from one configuration to the next, and ConfVolumes is the way to follow it:

commands.txt
firstConf 0
lastConf 2
stepConf 1

PostProcessor ParticleStress
Volume 1.0
ConfVolumes 3
0 0.0012
1 0.00121
2 0.00123

Tip

The six bounds of a box of driven walls are printed by see with the key x, which gives a quick way of building this list for a triaxial cell.

Note

For the stress of the soft particles themselves, which Rockable computes during the run, use the data extractors TrackSoftParticle or TrackStress (see Data extractors).

SampleState

The state of the whole sample, one line per processed configuration, in the file SampleState.txt: the time, the sizes and the volume of the sample, the solid fraction, the coordination number, the number of contacts, and the stress tensor computed from the contact forces.

commands.txt
firstConf 0
lastConf 100
stepConf 1

PostProcessor SampleState
WallBox 0 1 2 3 4 5

Keyword

Description

WallBox <xmin> <xmax> <ymin> <ymax> <zmin> <zmax>

The numbers of the six walls of a box. The sizes of the sample are the distances between their inner faces (the position of each wall, minus or plus its Minkowski radius times its homothety).

Volume <double>

A volume imposed for every configuration (the sizes are then written as 0, or taken from the box or the cell if there is one).

With a periodic cell, nothing has to be given: the volume is \(\det \mathbf{h}\), and the sizes are the lengths of the three vectors of the cell.

The columns of SampleState.txt are:

Column

Content

1

time

2-4

sizes \(L_x\), \(L_y\), \(L_z\) of the sample

5

volume \(V\)

6

solid fraction: the volume of the free particles (the volume of their shape times the cube of their homothety) divided by \(V\)

7

coordination number \(Z\): twice the number of contacts between free bodies plus the number of contacts with driven bodies, divided by the number of free bodies. Two bodies touching at several points (vertex-face, edge-edge…) make one contact.

8

number of contacts (pairs of bodies)

9, 10

mean stress \(p = \operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma}/3\) and von Mises stress \(q\)

11-16

\(\sigma_{xx}\), \(\sigma_{yy}\), \(\sigma_{zz}\), \(\sigma_{xy}\), \(\sigma_{xz}\), \(\sigma_{yz}\)

The stress is

\[\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \frac{1}{V} \sum_{p} \sum_{c} \underline{F}_c \otimes \left( \underline{x}_p - \underline{x}_c \right)\]

where the first sum runs over the free bodies, the second over their contacts, \(\underline{F}_c\) is the force received by the body \(p\) at the contact point \(\underline{x}_c\). It is positive in compression, and only its symmetric part is written. It does not include the kinetic contribution of the velocity fluctuations, negligible in a quasi-static state. With a periodic cell, it is the opposite of the stress that Rockable computes during the computation to drive the cell (which is positive in tension).

Note

With walls, the contact forces with the walls are included through the free bodies that touch them: the stress is that of the sample. In a box driven by a servo-control, \(\sigma_{xx}\), \(\sigma_{yy}\) and \(\sigma_{zz}\) match the imposed pressure once the sample is at equilibrium.

ClusterGranulo

Distribution of the sizes of the sub-clusters, for fragmentation studies: as the interfaces break, an initial cluster splits into sub-clusters, and this post-processor counts them.

Keyword

Description

SievingSizes <nb>

Followed by nb values, in increasing order, one per class: the largest number of particles of a sub-cluster in that class.
commands.txt
firstConf 0
lastConf 50
stepConf 10

PostProcessor ClusterGranulo
SievingSizes 5
1 2 3 4 5

The results are written into ClusterGranulo-conf<iconf>.txt, one line per class: the value given in SievingSizes, and the fraction (in number) of the sub-clusters in that class.

A sub-cluster of \(n\) particles goes into the first class whose value is not smaller than \(n\), and the sub-clusters larger than the last value go into the last class. With 1 2 3 4 5, the classes hold the sub-clusters of 1, 2, 3 and 4 particles, and of 5 particles or more; with 1 10 100, the single particles, the sub-clusters of 2 to 10 particles, and those of more than 10. It is a distribution of the number of particles per sub-cluster, not a grain-size distribution.

Note

The sub-clusters are identified from the interfaces that are still intact, so this analysis is only meaningful together with a force law that breaks interfaces (StickedLinks or BCM). The file of a configuration without any sub-cluster is left empty.

Post-processing without postpro

The conf-files are plain text, and their format is documented in Format of configuration files (conf-file). For a one-off analysis, reading them with a short script is often quicker than writing a new post-processor. The Particles and Interactions blocks are column-formatted, and the number of entries is given on the line that opens each block.

Writing a new post-processor in C++ is described in Extending Rockable.
